# Last Mile Out

One line: an interactive evacuation planning rehearsal that reveals who a community plan leaves behind.

## The Unique Kernel
When a road closes or a resource changes, the map visibly recomputes which households can reach a suitable shelter and which cannot. The result is an actionable gap list, with reasons, so an organizer can improve a plan before a disaster.

## Who It's For
A community organizer or local emergency planning volunteer rehearsing support for residents with different mobility and transport needs. They need to see whether routes, vehicles, and accessible shelter capacity actually cover their neighborhood.

## The Core Loop
Open a fictional neighborhood, run a proposed evacuation, inspect households that cannot be assigned a viable route and shelter, adjust one resource or closure, run it again, and take away a list of remaining gaps.

## Inspiration & Identity
The learner asked for an extraordinary, real project that judges could remember. The memorable screen is the neighborhood map changing from apparently covered to visibly stranded households when a road closes.

## Why This Matters to the Learner
They want a stronger, substantially different hackathon entry with design, impact, innovation, and presentation potential.

## What “Working” Looks Like
In a short demo, the organizer runs one scenario. A closure strands a resident who needs step-free transport and a suitable shelter. Changing a vehicle or shelter constraint updates the assignment and the uncovered-needs list. Every assignment has a plain-language reason.

## The POC Boundary
One fictional neighborhood and one planning drill; editable road closures, vehicle availability, and shelter accessibility or capacity; transparent route and assignment logic; a map and accessible text list; exportable planning gaps. The demo requires no account, paid API, or live emergency data.

## Later
Importing verified local road and shelter data, participatory neighborhood data collection, multi-organizer review, and drills for other hazards.

## Explicitly Cut
Live evacuation directions and emergency alerts: those need verified, current data and formal operational partnerships. Predictions of flood or road conditions: the organizer supplies a scenario. Claims of actual safety: the app is a planning rehearsal using fictional data.
